Points and lines The cross product a×b of vectors a = (x1, y1) and b = (x2, y2) is calculated using the formula x1y2 −x2y1. The cross product tells us whether b turns left (positive value), does not turn (zero) or turns right (negative value) when it is placed directly after a. The following picture illustrates the above cases: a b a× b = 6 a b a× b = 0 a b a× b = −8 For example, in the ﬁrst case a = (4,2) and b = (1,2). The following code calculates the cross product using the class complex: P a = {4,2}; P b = {1,2}; C p = (conj(a)*b).Y; // 6 The above code works, because the function conj negates the y coordinate of a vector, and when the vectors (x1,−y1) and (x2, y2) are multiplied together, the y coordinate of the result is x1y2 −x2y1. Point location Cross products can be used to test whether a point is located on the left or right side of a line. Assume that the line goes through points s1 and s2, we are looking from s1 to s2 and the point is p. For example, in the following picture, p is on the left side of the line: s1 s2 p The cross product (p −s1)×(p −s2) tells us the location of the point p. If the cross product is positive, p is located on the left side, and if the cross product is negative, p is located on the right side. Finally, if the cross product is zero, points s1, s2 and p are on the same line.
