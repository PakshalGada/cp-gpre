An Eulerian circuit is an Eulerian path that starts and ends at the same node. For example, the graph has an Eulerian circuit that starts and ends at node 1: 1. 2. 3. 4. 5. 6. Existence The existence of Eulerian paths and circuits depends on the degrees of the nodes. First, an undirected graph has an Eulerian path exactly when all the edges belong to the same connected component and • the degree of each node is even or • the degree of exactly two nodes is odd, and the degree of all other nodes is even. In the ﬁrst case, each Eulerian path is also an Eulerian circuit. In the second case, the odd-degree nodes are the starting and ending nodes of an Eulerian path which is not an Eulerian circuit. For example, in the graph nodes 1, 3 and 4 have a degree of 2, and nodes 2 and 5 have a degree of 3. Exactly two nodes have an odd degree, so there is an Eulerian path between nodes 2 and 5, but the graph does not contain an Eulerian circuit.
