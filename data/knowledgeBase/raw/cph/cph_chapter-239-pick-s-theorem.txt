Pick’s theorem Pick’s theorem provides another way to calculate the area of a polygon provided that all vertices of the polygon have integer coordinates. According to Pick’s theorem, the area of the polygon is a+ b/2−1, where a is the number of integer points inside the polygon and b is the number of integer points on the boundary of the polygon. For example, the area of the polygon (4,1) (7,3) (5,5) (2,4) (4,3) is 6+7/2−1 = 17/2. Distance functions A distance function deﬁnes the distance between two points. The usual distance function is the Euclidean distance where the distance between points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) is q (x2 −x1)2 +(y2 −y1)2. An alternative distance function is the Manhattan distance where the distance between points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) is |x1 −x2|+|y1 −y2|.
